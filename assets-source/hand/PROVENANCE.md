# Authored hand source and rig

The runtime no longer uses the authored hand. Since 2026-11-07 it renders the standard WebXR generic-hand meshes (`../xr-hands/PROVENANCE.md`), and `public/assets/hand.glb`, `src/xr/rigged-hand.ts` and its tests were removed. This folder is kept as the record of the authored hands; a later slim-v1 attempt lives on the unmerged `hand-slim-v1` branch.

## long-fingers-v1 (last served)

long-fingers-v1 was the last authored runtime hand. The staged GLB (`authored-hand.staged.glb`), the then-served `public/assets/hand.glb` and `rig-metadata.json` are copies of `long-fingers-v1/authored-hand.staged.glb`. GLB SHA-256 is `e143187b1f577574e158286f063d77f6d00fd09e8887dd70db4190cf606f4eb6`; the runtime requests `/assets/hand.glb?v=e143187b1f57`.

The owner scrapped the precision line (rig-v2 through precision-v6) and returned to the first authored hand, built by `rig_hand.py` and `animate_hand.py` from his skin-modifier source mesh (see Initial rig history). Its fingers were too short and bulbous at the ends to easily use the Keyborb. At his request the 14-vertex `Hand.001` source was edited (`.local/tools/blend/long_fingers.py`: index, middle and outer chains 25% longer, every tip radius 0.95 of its mid radius, each tip moved out by the radius it lost) in `vr-cartoon-hand2-long-fingers.blend` to lengthen the fingers and thumb and narrow the tips, and approved the result in Blender. That file is copied unmodified as `long-fingers-v1/source.blend` (SHA-256 `5a98ecddd51aca6dbfe4a127dae54563d716669098a8bc9fb30f4801bdc7828e`). Its `Hand.001_original` object is an unused earlier copy.

The rig and weight logic is the first hand's: 13 bones, four clips (Open, Fist, Pointing, Thumbs Up), no thumb helper bones and no `thumb_fold_*` morphs. The only pipeline changes are that every bone carries `restLength` in metres as a glTF extra, the source's earlier `Armature` actions are dropped from the build session so they cannot collide with or leak into the export, the export is asserted (one skin of 13 joints, the four clips, every `restLength`), and the material is set to the orange every served hand has used (`[0.7989, 0.1449, 0.0085]`), because the owner's source file shades `Hand.001` blue. The rigged scene is written to `long-fingers-v1/rigged-hand.blend` (SHA-256 `766ad11fb04b9e29cabfbbaf9e1f82cf61ed414be1ef72306c86240a67be06db`).

Compared with the first hand (commit d4ebf2f, measured the same way on both reimported GLBs in mm): middle reach from the wrist 203.8 to 236.1, index 202.6 to 237.9, outer 204.9 to 238.0, thumb 166.0 to 179.6. The tip width across the last 10 mm is index 42.7 to 32.5, middle 42.1 to 32.4 and outer 43.9 to 31.8. Bone chains are thumb 65.6 to 82.4, index 71.0 to 104.4, middle 72.3 to 103.5 and outer 68.1 to 100.7. The finger roots are unchanged, about 50 mm apart. The longer skin evaluates to 4,130 vertices and 8,256 triangles (first hand: 2,850 and 5,696) with the same modifiers and edge graph. Blender reimport confirmed 13 bones, the four clips, 1–4 influences per vertex, finite weights summing to between 0.99999986 and 1.00000011, and `restLength` on all 13 bones. Two builds from the same source produced byte-identical GLBs.

Without the precision-v6 helpers and bind, the thumb root crimps on the captured thumb slides (17–28 flipped blend-band triangles against v6's 0–2), and on the captured fists 64–65 thumb-root triangles flip, covering 347–419 mm² (v13: 0–2 triangles, at most 3.4 mm²). The first hand under today's runtime flips 343–389 mm² on the same fists, so the crimp is inherited; no test covers the fist case, and the longer thumb's pad rests about 22 mm past the tracked fist contact (v13: about 5). `tests/rigged-hand.test.ts` records both bars. Headset review remains owner acceptance.

Regenerate from `assets-source/hand/long-fingers-v1`:

```bash
blender source.blend --background --python-exit-code 1 --python rig_hand.py --python animate_hand.py
```

Then copy `authored-hand.staged.glb` to `../authored-hand.staged.glb` and `../../../public/assets/hand.glb`, copy `rig-metadata.json` to `..`, and set `RIGGED_HAND_ASSET_URL` in `src/xr/rigged-hand.ts` to the first 12 hex digits of the GLB SHA-256. See `long-fingers-v1/README.md`.

## Precision line history

The thumb is re-bound along the mirror-averaged tracked neutral thumb direction, so `thumb_01` starts about 18° from bind at the neutral pose instead of 68–75°. The bind was baked with dual-quaternion skinning, the actions were re-keyed to keep their poses, the wrist/`thumb_01` blend was widened to 60 mm and tapered off 25 mm behind the thumb head, and baked pits on the back of the hand were lifted out. Three skin-only helper bones at the `thumb_01` head (`thumb_root_helper`, `thumb_web_helper`, `thumb_heel_helper`; wrist children, 16 bones in all) carry the wrist/`thumb_01` band at half the thumb turn and the heel at the thumb root's travel; the runtime drives them from `thumb_01` (`THUMB_HELPERS` in `src/xr/rigged-hand.ts`). The root and web helpers are keyed at half the `thumb_01` key rotation in all four clips; the heel helper has no clip tracks because the clips carry no root travel. On the right-hand slide capture and its true mirror on the left hand, the flipped root triangles fall from 10–11 to 0 (open palm) and 8 to at most 2 (closed fingers). Two per-side corrective shape keys, `thumb_fold_right` (461 vertices, at most 8.4 mm) and `thumb_fold_left` (397 vertices, at most 6.6 mm), each fitted by `precision-v6/fold_morph.ts` on that hand's captures and added by `corrective.py`, round the closed-fist thumb root back toward precision-v5's outline. Each hand drives only its own key, weighted by the folded thumb's closure-weighted root travel (`THUMB_FOLD_START`/`THUMB_FOLD_FULL` in `src/xr/rigged-hand.ts`) and eased with an 85 ms time constant (`THUMB_FOLD_SMOOTHING`), with each frame's change capped by the skin motion under the key (`THUMB_FOLD_SKIN_RATIO`), so it is 0 on the open palm, at neutral, on a pinch and in controller mode and does not follow tracking jitter. They reduce the knob and pit on the thumb-side edge of both fists to a low ledge and keep the right fist's palm-side tip seen from above at 4.4 mm past v5 (11.3 before the corrective). Since v13 the right key's fit also fills the ledge's corner 10–18 mm above the thumb head toward the outline's concave envelope, halving the right fist's back-edge dip (5.7–6.5 to 2.8–3.0 mm). The left key does not, because every left fill added flips on the real left fists of the motion capture. The ledge, the fold line at the right thumb-side root and 42–43 ml of closed-fist volume loss (v5: 30) remain and await headset review. See `precision-v6/README.md`.

## Quest neutral calibration

On November 5, 2026, Paul authorized a live open-hand capture from the attached Quest. Each side's reference averages 12 stable frames from that capture. `tests/fixtures/quest-neutral-hands.json` stores the mean joint transforms normalized to the wrist, without room coordinates or device identifiers. `src/xr/hand-neutral-reference.json` stores the derived palm-relative joint quaternions. These are owner-specific neutral references, not a change to the Blender mesh, rig or editing groups.

For each tracked bone, the renderer computes the current palm-relative rotation times the inverse neutral rotation and applies that delta to an aligned rest orientation: the authored rest re-aimed by the minimal swing that points the bone along the neutral joint's -Z axis. It then removes the current parent world rotation. The authored rest pose differs from the tracked neutral by 15–33 degrees on the fingers and 67–75 degrees on the thumb (authored sideways, tracked forward beside the index). Applying deltas to the unaligned rest made the thumb a sideways paddle in every unopposed pose; that was the main defect in the November 5 headset recording. Parent-first updates prevent thumb base motion from bending the next thumb joints a second time. Child-bone translations and all scales remain fixed. Corrective motion can move only the thumb root, within an explicit bound. This uses the joint orientations defined by the [WebXR Hand Input specification](https://immersive-web.github.io/webxr-hand-input/), retaining twist that a position-only segment direction cannot represent.

Regression checks require every bone to lie within 1 degree of its tracked joint axis for the neutral captures and for unopposed recorded Quest thumbs, and verify rigid thumb base motion without extra child bends. Physical Quest appearance and pinch comfort remain owner acceptance.

The visible thumb starts at the palm-edge MCP joint, not the anatomical metacarpal joint inside the palm. Both `thumb_01` and `thumb_02` therefore use the tracked `thumb-phalanx-proximal` rotation and its matching neutral reference. Together they form the 39.51 mm proximal section. `thumb_03` uses `thumb-phalanx-distal`, retaining the tip hinge at x=110 mm and a 20.01 mm distal section. Independent Blender inspection confirmed the root at x=70.67 mm and strong thumb-root weights at the palm junction, so the mesh, weights and rig remain unchanged. Regressions move the proximal joint while holding the metacarpal still, reproducing the previous halfway bend on both hands before verifying the corrected palm-edge hinge.

## Captured gesture constraints

A subsequent Quest video and joint capture showed that rotation transfer alone left large gaps: the source Fist action retained approximately 22–23 mm between fingers, and captured physical pinches separated by 3–5 mm rendered approximately 8 cm apart. The source geometry and rig are unchanged. Tracked fingers follow their joints; the coordinated closed pose only shapes the controller grip. Palm cupping swings the index and outer digits toward the middle finger as all three fingers close; their roots no longer translate, because moving a skinned root bunched the palm skin. During opposition only, thumb-root movement follows the captured MCP displacement scaled by the opposition weight, and a stable-pole, two-link solver swings the thumb from its tracked direction toward the rendered index pad or, on a closed hand, toward the matching point of whichever index or middle phalanx the tracked thumb tip touches, at the tracked offset direction. The tracked tip joint sits at the skin while the thumb bone ends inside its pad (5.4 mm short, measured from the mesh at load), so a resting thumb aims its pad surface at that point; aiming the bone end left the thumb straight at full reach and its tip about 5 mm above the fist. The open-hand pinch keeps its bone-end target. The previous fixed folded-knuckle target and a spurious pinch share in fists placed the resting thumb on the wrong segment in 85% of captured fist frames.

The neutral reference includes wrist/palm-relative thumb-base positions. Root travel is bounded to 50 mm for pinch opposition and 30 mm for a folded thumb, and the skin-only thumb helper bones follow a share of it; the index and outer roots do not travel. Segment lengths and scales remain unchanged. Raw joint transforms and input positions are never modified. The rendered fingertip midpoint continues to guide Keyborb targeting.

`tests/fixtures/quest-gesture-poses.json` contains wrist-normalized bilateral pinch, partial-closure and held full-fist captures. The earlier `captured-close` right-hand sample remains explicitly partial. The later `captured-fist` set averages five stable tracked frames per hand while the owner held both fists closed with the thumbs across the fingers. Tests require surface gaps below 14 mm for both full fists (the authored roots sit about 45 mm apart, so tracked curl plus cupping does not close them fully), near-contact rendered pinch pads through wrist rotations, and unchanged source tracking. Browser replay includes front and opposing side views. Headset appearance, continuous motion and typing comfort remain owner acceptance.

## Initial rig history

It preserves the user's editable edge mesh, Skin and Subdivision modifiers, material, and scene data. SHA-256: `e9ab1bd430680efac0269d759a541b93d5966043834e4caf4f2a7f6964d387fe`.

The script retains the original object hidden, bakes its evaluated surface, constructs the skeleton and weights, and exports `authored-hand.staged.glb` and `rig-metadata.json`.
